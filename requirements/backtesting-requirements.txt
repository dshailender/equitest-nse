# Product Requirements Document (PRD)
**Project:** Quantitative Backtesting Framework for Indian Equities  
**Document Owner:** Senior Financial Research Analyst / Product Owner  
**Target Audience:** Quantitative Development and Data Engineering Teams  

## 1. Overview
This document outlines the requirements for building a robust, parameter-driven backtesting framework for Indian equities. The initial implementation focuses on a specific trend-following strategy using Exponential Moving Averages (EMAs) and market regime filters, simulated over 5, 10, and 15-year periods. The platform must be scalable, heavily parameterized for scenario analysis, and free from common backtesting biases (survivorship, look-ahead).

## 2. Strategy Summary (The "Source of Truth")
*   **Trading Universe:** NSE 750 excluding the top 100 (i.e., NSE rank 101 to 750).
*   **Capital & Risk:** Starting corpus of ₹5,00,000. Maximum risk per trade is 2% of the corpus (₹10,000 initially). 
*   **Market Regime Filter:** Take trades only if the NIFTY index is trading above both its 50-day and 200-day EMAs.
*   **Stock Trend Filter:** EMA 20 > EMA 50 > EMA 150 > EMA 200.
*   **Price Action Filter:** Stock price must be > 0.85 × 52-week high (corrected from original 15% phrasing).
*   **Entry Rule:** Buy when the price breaks above the 20-day EMA.
*   **Stop Loss (SL):** 7% from the entry price. Position sizing must be derived from the 2% account risk and the 7% SL.
*   **Exit Rule (Profit Booking):** Sell when the price closes below the 20-day EMA.
*   **Simulation Periods:** Last 5, 10, and 15 years.
*   **Core Output:** Total capital growth, number of trades, average profit, average loss, win percentage.

## 4. Open Questions & Required Assumptions
The development team must proceed with the **Default Assumptions** listed below unless overridden by the Product Owner during Sprint Planning.

1. **Trade Execution Price & Look-ahead Bias:**
   * *Ambiguity:* The rule says "Buy when price breaks above EMA 20". Is this an intraday break or an End-of-Day (EOD) close? 
   * *Default Assumption:* To prevent look-ahead bias with EOD data, signals are generated if `Close(T)` crosses above `EMA20(T)`. The trade is executed at the **Open price on T+1**.
2. **Stop Loss Execution & Gap Down Risk:**
   * *Ambiguity:* What happens if a stock opens below the 7% stop loss on the next day (Gap down)? 
   * *Default Assumption:* The system will exit at the Open price of that day, resulting in a loss > 7% and a risk realization > 2%. The engine must simulate realistic market gaps.
3. **Signal Clashing / Capital Allocation:**
   * *Ambiguity:* Since risking 2% at a 7% SL requires allocating ~28.5% of total capital per trade, the system can hold a maximum of 3-4 concurrent trades. What if 10 stocks give a signal on the same day?
   * *Default Assumption:* Sort daily signals by highest momentum (e.g., Distance of Close from EMA20) or randomize selection. *Product Owner needs to finalize ranking criteria for tied signals.*
4. **Historical Universe (Survivorship Bias):**
   * *Ambiguity:* How do we accurately define NSE 101-750 for 15 years ago?
   * *Default Assumption:* The data vendor must provide historical point-in-time constituent lists. If unavailable, use the current NSE 750 list as a fallback, but flag the results as containing survivorship bias.
